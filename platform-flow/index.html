Room = car capacity (230) − predicted load + predicted alighting. Ratio = people queuing or walking to that door ÷ room. Under 0.7 is green, under 1.0 is amber, 1.0 or more is red. A door whose queue area holds more than 63 people turns red regardless, so the lights never send people into a crowd.

How the system works

  1. Collect

    Queue counts per door from platform cameras, load per car of the approaching train, and the historical alighting table by station, day and hour.

  2. Predict

    Forecast how many people leave each car, and so how much room each door will have when the train opens.

  3. Recommend

    Compare that room with the people already queuing or walking to each door, and rank the doors.

  4. Update lights

    Set the tunnel signs and door lights to green, amber or red. Refresh every 2 seconds as people move.

  5. Evaluate

    Measure queue evenness, people left behind and waiting time, and feed each train's real alighting back into the model.

Where the AI does real work

  • Forecasting: the alighting model blends the historical average with corrections learned online from every train, and its error falls as it sees more trains.
  • Decision: each light is chosen from predicted room, not from the current queue alone, so a door that looks crowded now can stay green if many people are about to get off there.
  • Anti-herding: passengers who follow the lights are spread across green doors in proportion to spare room, so one green door does not become the next hotspot.

What this prototype assumes

  • Passenger counts, train loads and the historical table are simulated. In a pilot they would come from CCTV people-counting, car weight sensors and gate data.
  • Only a share of people follow the lights. Drag the slider to 0% to check that the right side then behaves like the left.
  • The lights advise passengers and staff. They do not replace platform staff, platform screen doors or any safety system.
