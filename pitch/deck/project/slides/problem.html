The problem

Rush hour packs a few doors while others stay empty

People queue by their tunnel. Passengers stop at the doors nearest to where they walked onto the platform.

They can’t see ahead. At Admiralty, the platform and the train are hidden until you leave the corridor.

Space goes unused. Some doors overflow and people miss the train, while doors further along still have room.

Our simulation, Monday 09:00: without guidance, doors 2A, 3A and 6A overflow beside the tunnels.

0:15–0:45 (30 s). At rush hour, people queue at the doors closest to where they come onto the platform. At stations like Admiralty, you can’t see the platform or the train until you leave the corridor, so you can’t make a better choice. The result: a few doors overflow and people miss the train, while other doors along the same platform still have space. The capacity is there; it just isn’t used evenly.
